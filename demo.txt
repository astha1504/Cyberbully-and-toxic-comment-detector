30-SECOND PITCH

Pehra is a safety layer for student communities. Our Pehra Safety Loop helps people pause before harmful posts, understand suspicious messages across text, screenshots, and voice notes, then follow a relevant next step. People see the warning signs and reach an official service or request consent-based review by a community safety moderator. Pehra supports safer decisions. It does not decide guilt or file a police complaint.

JUDGE QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

1. What problem does Pehra solve?

Online communities face both harmful user interactions and scam messages shared through calls, SMS, WhatsApp, screenshots, and voice notes. People often need a clear explanation and next step before they decide what to do. Pehra combines an early posting intervention with India-focused scam triage and a private case follow-up flow.

2. What is the unique selling point?

Our USP is an India-focused safety workflow for student communities. It combines prevention while people write, repeat-behavior moderation, explainable scam checks across text, screenshots, and voice notes, and situation-specific next steps. Users can move from a warning to an official reporting service or consent-based moderator review. We call this the Pehra Safety Loop: pause, understand, respond, connect. This is a distinctive product combination we are building, not a claim that no other service offers related features. Our pilot will test whether this combination improves user decisions.

3. How is Pehra different from a government reporting portal?

Pehra helps people recognize warning signs and prepare a safer response. NCRP handles cybercrime complaints, and Chakshu accepts reports about suspected fraud communications. Pehra does not replace either service. A financial fraud victim should report through 1930 or NCRP. A suspicious call, SMS, or WhatsApp message should go to Chakshu. A negative match in an identifier search does not prove safety because the official repository says records can be incomplete or contain errors.

4. What happens in a live scan?

The user pastes a message or supplies a screenshot or voice note. Screenshot text goes through Tesseract OCR. Voice goes to the configured Groq transcription service, then the transcript is checked. The scanner applies explainable text patterns, returns matching warning signals, and provides relevant safety steps and official links. The app does not open links, install files, or inspect an APK for malware. The result is a warning, not proof of fraud.

5. How does the posting intervention work in real time?

While a user types, the frontend waits briefly, then asks the API to check the draft. A warning shows matched wording and available category signals. The user chooses to edit, cancel, or continue. Accepted posts and comments also receive server-side moderation, which can finish in the background. Chat checks the message before delivery and offers a review choice. The third confirmed moderation finding triggers a 24-hour posting mute.

6. What real-world impact do you have so far?

The current evidence is a working prototype and automated tests, not a field study. We have not measured prevented fraud, reduced harassment, or user outcomes yet. A college pilot should track edit and continue rates, confirmed false positives and misses, time to an appropriate official service, repeat violations, user trust, and appeal outcomes. We will report measured results after a reviewed pilot.

7. Who is the first user and what is the scope?

The first users are student communities, colleges, and online groups that need safer discussion and basic scam awareness. The current prototype checks posts, comments, chat, image text, voice transcripts, and user-submitted scam messages. Text patterns include English, Hindi, Hinglish, and selected Marathi, Bengali, Tamil, and Telugu phrases. Coverage is limited and needs broader language testing.

8. How does Pehra scale?

The current build uses a React frontend, FastAPI service, and MongoDB. It is a local prototype, not a deployed high-availability service. A production plan would add stateless API replicas behind a load balancer, queued workers for OCR and transcription, encrypted object storage for evidence, rate limits, monitoring, backups, retention controls, and load testing. We would measure latency and error rates before increasing rollout size.

9. How would the startup earn revenue?

A plausible first model is a paid service for colleges and community platforms, with admin tools, configurable moderation policies, and safety reporting workflows. We have not validated buyer demand, pricing, or sales yet. The next step is a small pilot with student and safety reviewers, followed by evidence on usefulness and operating cost.

10. How do you handle false positives and missed threats?

The scanner uses patterns and is not a calibrated probability model. Text moderation also depends on the available model and explicit phrase rules. OCR can misread images, and transcription can mishear speech. We show the detected evidence, avoid claiming certainty, and direct users to official guidance. A reviewed dataset and human evaluation are required before claiming accuracy.

11. What data do you keep?

The scan endpoint analyzes submitted content without creating a case. A user who wants follow-up must separately consent to a private case, and evidence upload asks for separate consent. Case records and evidence expire through a 30-day MongoDB TTL policy. Reporters and allowlisted moderators have application-level access, and evidence access is recorded in the case history. The history is not a tamper-proof external audit log. Evidence receives file-type and size checks, not malware scanning. These controls need a security review before production use.

12. Is Pehra connected to police, Chakshu, NCRP, or Pratibimb?

No. Pehra links users to official services and helps prepare information. Users submit through the official service themselves. Pratibimb is an I4C Samanvaya module for law-enforcement agencies. Pehra has no access to it and sends it no data.

13. Is this a women-safety product?

Safer posting, reporting, and case follow-up support safer online communities, including women and students. The current prototype does not provide emergency response, live location sharing, or law-enforcement intervention. We need feedback from women-safety organizations before making broader safety claims or adding those workflows.

14. What remains before a startup launch?

Run a supervised college pilot, measure language-specific accuracy, review false positives and misses, complete a threat model and independent security review, test load and recovery, improve evidence storage and malware checks, and define support and deletion operations. Validate a buyer and pricing model before describing the business as proven.

15. What should judges remember?

Pehra links prevention, explanation, and action. It helps a user pause before sending harmful content, see why a suspicious message raised concern, and reach the appropriate official channel. The next milestone is a measured pilot with student users and safety reviewers.

HONEST FEATURE DESCRIPTION

The local text model is binary. Category percentages come from phrase rules, not a multi-label probability model. OCR detects readable text, not image meaning. Voice moderation checks a provider transcript. Post and comment moderation finish in a background task, so their status often stays pending briefly. Analytics counts comments. The Behaviour page records server-confirmed moderation findings and applies a 24-hour mute from the third finding.
